## Decision
1. **Database-side alarms are evaluated inside Postgres** by `platform.check_alarms()` (pg_cron, every minute). It
   records state changes in `platform.alarm_events` (firing → resolved):
   - `dlq-depth`: any message in a `*_dlq` queue (conventions §5)
   - `relay-lag`: oldest unpublished outbox row > 5 min (conventions §7)
   - `queue-lag`: oldest message in an event or work queue > 5 min (insight LLD)
   - `job-failed`: a pg_cron job failed in the last 10 min
   - `invoke-failed`: a scheduler call to a service timed out or got a 4xx/5xx
2. **Notification:** if the Vault secret `alarm_webhook_url` is set, each state change is POSTed as JSON (environment,
   alarm, service, subject, value; never PII). Any webhook works (Slack/Google Chat incoming webhook, an email relay).
   The product owner picks one in the provisioning session. Without it, alarms can be seen in the table (Studio / SQL).
3. **HTTP SLO alarms** (5xx > 2% over 5 min, p95 above target over 10 min) need a metrics backend. Services emit
   OpenTelemetry metrics (F-12). The backend (e.g. Grafana Cloud free tier) and its alert rules are chosen in the
   provisioning session. Until then they are covered only by the Vercel dashboard. Recorded as a provisioning item and
   a paid-gate item (REL-01).

## Why
- No extra vendor for the pilot, and everything is in Mumbai (BRD A-10). The data lives next to the queues and outboxes
  it checks.
- The webhook payload has no PII, so it's safe for any chat tool.

## Impact
- F-15 is delivered with the database-side alarms. The HTTP SLO alarm wiring moves to the provisioning session (with F-07).
- Runbook `provisioning.md` gains the "configure scheduler and alarms" step (`infra/scripts/configure-environment.mjs`).
- No change to contracts, schemas of services, or later-stage tasks.
